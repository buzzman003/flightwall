The shopping material below is retained as a deferred research record only.

1. IDENTIFY AND PREPARE THE HOST
Confirm the device model, available storage, existing workloads and Ethernet.
Prefer Home Assistant OS on supported dedicated hardware; an existing always-on
VM host is another option. Choose the installation guide only after identification.
Identify the exact target disk and preserve its data before any image write.
Do not install HA on the Batocera games computer as part of this plan. [1]
Establish reliable power, automatic startup, clock synchronization, a stable LAN
address and private HA backups. Keep its administrator interface off public ports.
Record the actual HA/core/integration versions; review differences from the pins
below before relying on their behavior. Amazon list access still needs internet.

WINDOWS PILOT AND HARDWARE CHOICES (NOT SELECTED)
If the existing desktop has capacity, first evaluate HAOS in a VM without buying
hardware. A proposed pilot allocation is 2 vCPUs, 4 GB RAM and an expanding 64 GB
virtual disk; this is a candidate configuration, not a verified installation.
HA documents VirtualBox, VMware Workstation and Hyper-V; its VM minimum is 2 GB
RAM and 2 vCPUs. Use UEFI and bridged networking. CPU virtualization must be
enabled. Windows Home needs VirtualBox or another compatible hypervisor; the
full Hyper-V role requires a supported Pro/Enterprise edition. Hyper-V has no
USB passthrough. Check available capacity and existing VM software first. [10,11]
The desktop must stay awake with automatic VM startup; sleep, Windows updates
and restarts interrupt HA. Measure actual electricity use rather than inferring
it from the power-supply rating. A verified HA backup can later move to another
supported host. No Windows settings or virtualization software were changed. [12]

Current US Green MSRP is $199 before tax/shipping, including power supply and
Ethernet cable, with 4 GB RAM and 32 GB eMMC. Official consumption is about
1.7 W idle / 3 W under load. At an illustrative $0.20/kWh, that is roughly
$0.25-$0.44 per 30-day month before power-supply losses and accessories. [13]
One DIY reference is a Pi 5 4 GB starter kit listed at $169.95 before tax/shipping:
case, active cooler, power supply, 64 GB microSD and reader are included; an
Ethernet cable may be extra. It has no SSD and requires assembly and HAOS flashing.
Recheck price and stock before purchase; compare complete kits, not bare boards.
HA supports Pi 4/5 with at least 2 GB RAM and 32 GB storage, preferably A2 SD. [14]
HA itself has no required subscription. Optional Nabu Casa hosted services list
US pricing of $6.50/month or $65/year plus tax. The Alexa Devices setup below
does not require that subscription. An iPad is a dashboard/controller client,
not a supported HA host; current Companion requirements include iOS/iPadOS 16.4+
and iPad fifth generation or newer, connecting to a separate HA server. [2,15,16]

EMAIL SNAPSHOT ALTERNATIVE TO CHECK BEFORE A PURCHASE
The owner confirmed that the existing Alexa shopping email contains grocery
item text, not only a link. An IFTTT email snapshot is therefore a candidate
to evaluate before buying HA hardware. No mailbox/sample was inspected here;
exact formatting, completeness and machine parsing still need a bounded test.
The original Amazon Alexa IFTTT integration was retired on October 31, 2023;
it cannot supply the old native shopping-list triggers or queries. [17]
IFTTT's October 28, 2026 guidance gives all plans, including Free, access to all
services and Applet features, including Webhooks, under usage credits. Successful
steps consume credits; Applets pause when the balance is exhausted. Review the
actual account allowance and step costs before claiming ongoing free operation.
Do not rely on older guidance that categorically requires Pro for Webhooks. [18]
IFTTT Email triggers expose subject/body fields. The owner's confirmation of
item text does not establish completeness after parsing. An email route shares
the chosen message with IFTTT and provide a dated snapshot, not verified ongoing
Amazon add/completion/deletion synchronization. Any future Flight Wall importer
still needs bounded parsing, a separate credential, stale/partial-state handling
and the signed-release gate below. No Applet, email forwarding or importer is
configured by this plan. [19]
Another candidate is connected Gmail's "New email matching search" trigger,
which accepts Gmail syntax including in:sent and exposes body_plain and message_id.
Verify the actual sender, mailbox and complete body first; a shopping email may
be in sent mail, but this is not established. Very long bodies are truncated,
so a matching message is not proof of a complete shopping snapshot. No new email
is sent and no Gmail connection is authorized or created by this plan. [20]

STORE-ARRIVAL EMAIL REQUEST (PROPOSED, NOT CONNECTED)
The owner wants an opted-in phone arriving at a configured grocery store to
request the Alexa shopping email automatically, once per Central calendar day
across all configured stores. Preserve the existing native Alexa shopping list.
A candidate chain is phone arrival -> shared daily reservation -> Alexa Routine
-> actual shopping email -> IFTTT text snapshot -> future Flight Wall importer.
IFTTT Location supports area-entry events on iPhone/Android, and its documented
Voice Monkey bridge can trigger an Alexa Routine. [21,22] Native Alexa location
events may avoid that bridge if available for the actual account/phone; inspect
the app before selecting this route. Independent per-store cooldowns do not
satisfy the shared daily limit. A strict gate needs persistent shared state.

FIRST PROOF: obtain the exact working spoken email command, put it in one Alexa
Routine Custom action without the wake word, and use one controlled Preview
with the owner choosing the recipient. Verify actual receipt with current list
items and no spoken follow-up. [23] Amazon's general Alexa+ Email to Contacts
workflow includes draft review and send confirmation. A successful conversation
does not prove an unattended Routine can complete that same action. [24]
Do not automate a blind yes or claim completion from an accepted command.

HA's send_text_command is an alternative request path, not a delivery guarantee.
The reviewed 2026.10.4/16.3.2 path uses legacy Alexa.TextCommand; maintainer notes
show Alexa+ Custom actions can use a different operation. The library queues
requests and does not return an email receipt. Target one physical Echo, not a
group, if tested. No HA purchase/install is justified solely by assuming this
email command works. [25,26] If the exact command requires interactive approval,
evaluate direct delivery of a verified native-list snapshot via a separately
configured email sender, or retain a user-confirmed export. Source-completeness
and freshness gates below still apply. No sending account is configured here.

For a production trigger, require the phone owner's own consent and tested
background permissions. Lisa's phone setup remains deferred; Chris's existing
400 m / 1,200 s preference must not silently be changed. Do not promise reliable
50 m arrivals from those reports. Test actual store boundaries, fresh/accurate
reports, drive-bys, locked-phone arrivals, duplicate events, restart persistence
and Central-Time midnight/DST. Reserve the daily attempt before external I/O;
ambiguous results must not trigger a second automatic email. Keep dispatch
metadata only, without GPS history. This remains a plan: no Routine, geofence,
message, IFTTT connection or new app release has been created by this research.

2. CONNECT THE BUILT-IN ALEXA DEVICES INTEGRATION
Use HA's built-in Alexa Devices integration for shopping, not Alexa Media Player.
The owner enters the intended Amazon account, password and authenticator-app OTP
privately in HA. The built-in flow does not request the authenticator seed. [2]
Confirm the Amazon region/account and discover the actual shopping-list entity;
do not guess an entity ID or select every household list. Expect reauthentication
and rate limits. Keep Amazon credentials in HA, never in Flight Wall or chat.
This HA-maintained integration reads Amazon's retail-site list service. It is
separate from Amazon's retired public skill List Management API. [3,8]
Ordinary native Alexa shopping additions can populate that list without invoking
Flight Wall; verify this on the owner's account before claiming a connection.

3. VERIFY SHOPPING DATA WITHOUT WRITING TO AMAZON
Call todo.get_items for the selected entity with status: needs_action. [4]
The response is keyed by entity ID: {entity_id: {items: [...]}}.
Alexa rows supply uid, summary and status (needs_action or completed). [5]
Map only uid and bounded summary into the proposed read-only mirror. The entity's
numeric state is an incomplete-item count, not its contents or freshness proof.
Observe an owner-created disposable item, rename, completion and deletion. The
adapter must never call add, update, remove or clear actions on Amazon's list.
Use item-change notifications, not count changes alone: renames and same-count
replacements matter. HA's todo/item/subscribe emits cached item arrays. [7]

4. RESOLVE SOURCE COMPLETENESS AND FRESHNESS BEFORE SHIPPING
Pinned review: HA core 2026.10.4 with aioamazondevices 16.3.2. [5,6,8]
todo.get_items serializes cached items; it does not fetch Amazon. Alexa list items
use initial synchronization and push updates; the normal five-minute device poll
and homeassistant.update_entity do not provide list reconciliation. [2,5,6]
Initial synchronization is best-effort. A failed initial fetch may leave missing
cache represented as an empty list. Generic device availability is not proof of
successful list initialization. A later push for one item is not a full baseline.
The pinned library fetches one page with limit=100 and has no pagination loop;
it also defaults a missing itemInfoList field to empty. Do not call such results
complete without checking response shape and pagination/size limits. [8]
Before enabling automatic replacement, use an upstream improvement or reviewed
HA-side adapter that exposes successful complete initialization and reconciliation.
It must distinguish valid empty results, partial results, failed requests, lost
push, authentication loss and throttling. Bound retries and reconcile periodically.
Cached reads and bridge heartbeats must not advance the Amazon-observation time.
Until this evidence exists, HA list visibility is a pilot, not a reliable TV mirror.

5. FUTURE FLIGHT WALL SHOPPING ADAPTER
Keep the existing Flight Wall list and native Alexa mirror in separate stores.
Offer an explicit ticker source: Flight Wall or native Alexa. Do not silently
merge lists, overwrite local entries or redirect existing skill list commands.
HA sends minimal snapshots over authenticated HTTPS with normal TLS validation.
Provision a separate revocable, write-only import credential; never reuse admin,
Alexa voice or phone credentials. It must grant no household-data reads.
Include list alias, source epoch/sequence, original observation time and explicit
success/completeness. Bound items/text; reject replay, future times and bad schema.
Only a proven successful complete snapshot removes absent IDs. Failures preserve
the stored snapshot while marking it unavailable; stale data must not look current.
Choose a documented freshness limit and hide the native ticker when stale/offline;
provide owner-visible status without exposing item names in maintenance logs.
Keep source IDs and mutable state in private DATA outside releases. Preserve
flight priority, ticker visibility controls and existing per-frame privacy checks.

6. ACCEPT SHOPPING BEFORE EXPANDING SCOPE
Test initialization failure versus empty success, missed push and reconciliation,
pagination/oversize rejection, same-count edits, duplicates, replay and stale expiry.
Verify native add/completion/deletion against HA and TV using disposable items;
measure latency without promising instantaneous delivery. Preserve unrelated items.
Test HA restart, internet loss, reauthentication and Batocera service/GUI handoff.
Confirm no Amazon mutation and no changes to the separate local Flight Wall list.

7. NATIVE TIMERS REMAIN A LATER, SEPARATE MIRROR
Amazon's skill timer permission covers that skill's own timers, not all native
timers. Built-in Alexa Devices exposes only the next timer per device. [2,9]
Community Alexa Media Player v5.18.0 / alexapy 1.33.0 exposes full returned lists,
but is unofficial and its ordinary poll can publish fresh-looking empty data when
a fetch returns None. Resolve that source-success ambiguity before implementation.
AMP also needs broader owner authentication, including its documented MFA seed.
See NATIVE-ALEXA-TIMERS.txt for pinned sources, schema and detailed acceptance gates.
Keep native timers separate from local timers; no Flight Wall voice cancellation
of native timers. Handle pause, stale data, multiple devices and more than five.

8. DELIVERY GATE
Do not modify installed 0.4.0 for a plan or HA account setup. Future app code must
use a new matching VERSION/module version above the latest published release.
Pass relevant tests and Linux native gates; build/sign/publish through the existing
workflow. Verify actual 0.4.0-to-new executable upgrade, rollback and TV behavior,
preserving games, private settings, original recovery helper and pinned trust key.

FLIGHT WALL 0.4.0 — HOUSEHOLD TOOLS AND YOUR PRIVATE ALEXA SKILL

WHAT THIS ADDS

You can tell your own Alexa device what is for dinner or add an appointment
to the Flight Wall display. For example:

  "Alexa, tell flight wall we are having teriyaki chicken for dinner."

For an appointment title, start a short conversation:

  "Alexa, open Flight Wall skill."
  "Add an appointment called Emerson doctor appointment."
  Answer the requested date and time, then confirm when Alexa reads it back.

With the optional kids rule enabled in household setup, dinner cards also
require a current Mom/Home custody state. See HOUSEHOLD-SETUP.txt for that rule.

These are local personal cards saved on your Batocera computer. They do not
create, change, or cancel Google Calendar events. Your read-only FC View flight
calendar continues to use its separate Google authorization. You can also
create personal cards from the terminal without Alexa.

The integration uses a standard custom Alexa skill and a small AWS Lambda
function. It does not use Alexa+ add-on APIs or require an Alexa+ subscription
as part of this design. Your Echo/account must support custom development
skills. Voice recognition, Amazon account/device support, and your live HTTPS
connection must still be tested on your own equipment.

Keep the skill in the Development stage for your own Amazon account. This
single-household implementation is not a public multi-user Skill Store app.
It is not necessary to submit the skill for public certification to test it
on your own compatible Alexa device registered to that same Amazon account.

SOURCE DEVELOPMENT / MATCHING DEPLOYMENTS

The 0.4.0 source adds timers, a shopping list and public flight watches. Local
source edits do not update the installed 0.3.0 Linux executable or the deployed
Lambda/model. Finish testing, build and sign a new versioned Linux release,
install it through the established update workflow, and deliberately deploy
the matching Alexa adapter/model before expecting these new routes to work.
Existing credentials and mutable household data stay outside releases.

BRIEF LAUNCH AND HOUSEHOLD COMMANDS

  "Alexa, open Flight Wall skill."
  Flight Wall: "Flight Wall. What would you like?"

Say help for the command choices. Dinner instructions are supplied only when
you ask for dinner help, not on every launch. Both appointment Title slots
use the same APPOINTMENT_TITLE type, as Amazon requires across intents.
Unlisted titles may be recognized, but custom slots are not unrestricted
dictation; verify real spoken examples on your own Echo.

Board timers:
  "Start a pasta timer for five minutes."
  "List timers."
  "Cancel the pasta timer." / "Cancel timer number two."

Labels have 1-80 characters; durations are whole seconds from one second to
24 hours. Up to five timers are displayed. Finished timers remain DONE until
dismissed, or until a newer timer needs their position. A sixth running timer
is rejected. UTC deadlines persist across service restarts and reboots; they
depend on a correct device clock. These are Flight Wall TV countdowns, not
ordinary Alexa system timers, Echo alarms or promised audible notifications.
The Alexa Timers API lists only timers created by the calling skill; this
adapter does not call it or request timer permissions. An ordinary command
such as "Alexa, set a timer" stays in Alexa and is not imported to Flight Wall.

Flight Wall shopping list:
  "Shopping list." / "Show the shopping list."
  "Add olives." / "Remove olives."
  "Read the shopping list."
  "Clear the shopping list." (Then explicitly confirm yes, or say no.)

Showing the list enables its persistent bottom ticker on the TV. This list
belongs to Flight Wall; it is separate from Amazon's built-in shopping list.
Items have 1-80 characters, with at most 100 items. Spoken readback includes
at most ten items and the remaining count. You can also explicitly invoke the
skill with "Alexa, ask Flight Wall to add olives" or "Alexa, ask Flight Wall
to show the shopping list". The phrases "Alexa, add olives to Flight Wall"
and "open Flight Wall shopping list" are unverified routing candidates;
Amazon may send them to native features. Do not promise they work without
testing. Opening Flight Wall skill, then giving the short command, avoids
relying on that unverified shortcut.

Public flight watch:
  "Track Delta flight one hundred twenty three on Friday."
  Check the airline, number and date in the readback, then say yes.

Missing or ambiguous airline, number and date values are requested, not guessed.
Common airline aliases map to DL (Delta), AA (American), UA (United), G4
(Allegiant) and F9 (Frontier). G9 means Air Arabia, not Allegiant. A watch is a
separate public-flight request; it neither exposes nor modifies the owner's
operating-calendar flights. If live data is not configured, Alexa says so.
Saving a watch does not establish that provider data or a live position exists.
Up to five requests for today through fourteen days ahead are retained in the
board timezone saved with each request. A repeated airline/number/date uses
the existing request. Past requested days leave the rotation automatically.
FlightAware access is optional, separately configured and potentially paid.
It shares the existing daily request cap; operating flights are checked first.
The lookup endpoint accepts dates only two days ahead, so more distant requests
wait locally without queries until their full requested day fits that window.
An exact airline/date match is required. Multiple legs or additional result
pages are shown as ambiguous instead of choosing a flight. No match or stale
data remains visibly unavailable; only positions at most five minutes old can
be shown as live. Requested dates refer to the board timezone, not an assumed
airport-local date. Specify the intended date accordingly for distant airports.


PART 1 — THE PIECES YOU WILL CONNECT

  Your voice / Echo
       -> Amazon Alexa custom skill
       -> Your AWS Lambda function
       -> Your Batocera HTTPS address, normally Tailscale Funnel
       -> Flight Wall's narrowly scoped household endpoints

Batocera and its Flight Wall service must be on and online when you speak.
The TV does not have to be showing Flight Wall at that moment. The saved card
will appear when you next open the app during the card's display window.

AWS needs a public HTTPS address. A private Tailscale Serve address alone is
not reachable from an ordinary Lambda function outside your Tailscale network.
Use the Funnel instructions in INSTALL.txt, Part 9. Do not expose SSH or add
router port-forwarding rules for this project.

Costs: AWS accounts and Lambda/CloudWatch usage can incur charges. Small
household use may fit the applicable free tier or account credits, but this
guide does not promise zero cost. Check your own AWS account's plan, credits,
billing settings, and current pricing before creating resources. Tailscale
availability and limits depend on your account as well.

Files supplied with this release:

  Flight-Wall-Alexa-Lambda-0.4.0.zip
      Ready to upload to AWS Lambda. lambda_function.py is at the ZIP root;
      bundled IANA timezone data provides a fallback for cloud runtimes.

  Flight-Wall-Alexa-Interaction-Model-en-US.json
      Upload this to the Alexa developer console's JSON editor.

  integrations/alexa/lambda_function.py
  integrations/alexa/interaction-model-en-US.json
  integrations/alexa/deployment-permission-example.json
      Source/reference copies in the installer and source download.

The ZIP and JSON above contain program code/model definitions, not credentials.
You will enter your own dedicated board token privately in the AWS console.
Do not put that token into the Python file, a repository, screenshots, or chat.


PART 2 — CHECK THE APP AND TRY A PERSONAL CARD LOCALLY

1. Install the 0.4.0 Batocera package using INSTALL.txt. If upgrading from
   0.1.0 or 0.2.0, close the Ports app before running the installer. It preserves your
   Google settings/tokens and original recovery helper. The upgrade is staged
   for the next Ports launch, rather than replacing the running program.

2. Open PORTS > Flight Wall once to activate the new version, then close it.
   This matters after an upgrade: the command-line launcher uses the selected
   current release, which remains the old version until that safe launch.

3. Connect to Batocera from your normal computer, using its actual IP:

   ssh root@192.168.1.50

   In the Batocera terminal, set the command shortcut and verify the version:

   FW=/userdata/roms/ports/.data/flightwall/flightwall
   "$FW" --version

   It should say 0.4.0 or a newer release with these household features.

4. Set your board's local timezone. This determines what "today" means and
   when a personal card appears:

   "$FW" config --set timezone America/Chicago
   "$FW" config --set personal_enabled true

   Choose another IANA timezone if appropriate. Do not leave the wall in UTC
   unless UTC is the local-day behavior you actually want for personal cards.

5. Try a dinner card without Alexa:

   "$FW" personal add --kind dinner --title 'Teriyaki chicken'

   With no date supplied, this uses today's date in the board's timezone.
   Dinner appears from 14:30 up to, but not including, 19:00 by default. Saving
   dinner at 10:00 is allowed; its card starts showing at 14:30. Saving today's
   dinner after 19:00 does not make it appear outside that window.

   To choose a particular date:

   "$FW" personal add --kind dinner --title 'Teriyaki chicken' --date 2026-11-03

   Adding a different dinner for the same date replaces that date's prior
   dinner card. The latest meal remains saved; it is not an extra duplicate.

6. An appointment needs a date and time. Replace this example date as needed:

   "$FW" personal add --kind appointment --title 'Emerson doctor appointment' --date 2026-11-03 --time 11:25 --duration-minutes 60

   The card is visible from that local day's midnight until the appointment
   ends. In this example it stops showing at 12:25, with no extra grace hour.
   An appointment without an explicit duration uses 60 minutes by default.

7. List saved cards or those visible right now:

   "$FW" personal list
   "$FW" personal list --visible

   To cancel one, use its actual ID from the list:

   "$FW" personal cancel PASTE_ITEM_ID_HERE

   Canceling a local card does not change any Google Calendar event.

8. If you prefer other display windows/defaults, set them locally:

   "$FW" config --set dinner_start 14:30
   "$FW" config --set dinner_end 19:00
   "$FW" config --set appointment_duration_minutes 60

   personal_enabled controls whether personal cards appear on the display;
   it does not prevent saving cards. Alexa will use the board's timezone and
   current defaults, rather than interpreting "today" as the Lambda server's
   UTC day. These defaults apply to newly created cards; already saved cards
   retain their saved display times. To change today's dinner window, set the
   defaults and save that date's dinner again. Restart the service after
   changing initial setup defaults.


PART 3 — START THE SERVICE AND CONFIGURE PUBLIC HTTPS

1. Close the Ports display first. Enable and start its background service:

   batocera-services enable flightwall
   batocera-services start flightwall

   Check the local health endpoint:

   curl http://127.0.0.1:8765/health

   The response should include "ok":true. If you chose a different local
   control_port, use that port consistently in the proxy target too.

2. Follow INSTALL.txt, Part 9, to install/sign into Tailscale and enable
   its optional flightwall_tailscale service. Then expose this app:

   TS=/userdata/system/configs/flightwall/tailscale/bin/tailscale
   TS_SOCKET=/userdata/system/configs/flightwall/tailscale/tailscaled.sock
   "$TS" --socket="$TS_SOCKET" funnel --bg http://127.0.0.1:8765

   Approve HTTPS/Funnel in your own Tailscale account when asked. If this
   HTTPS port currently uses private Serve, follow INSTALL.txt to reset that
   mode before enabling Funnel. Funnel deliberately makes this app's HTTPS
   endpoint public; its protected tools still need their own authentication.

3. Note the ACTUAL HTTPS origin printed by Tailscale. It resembles:

   https://flightwall.YOUR_TAILNET.ts.net

   The example is a placeholder. Close the display and set your actual origin:

   batocera-services stop flightwall
   "$FW" config --set public_url https://flightwall.YOUR_TAILNET.ts.net
   "$FW" config --set alexa_enabled true
   batocera-services start flightwall
   "$FW" alexa-info

   Use the hostname Tailscale actually assigned. public_url is the HTTPS
   origin only: no /mcp, no /api/v1 path, and no credentials in the URL.

4. On your normal computer or phone using another internet connection, open:

   https://YOUR_ACTUAL_HOSTNAME/health

   This should show the app's small public health response. Do not proceed
   to AWS setup if the public health page cannot reach your board. Testing
   from a phone on cellular data is useful because it avoids relying on your
   private home/Tailscale connection by accident.

5. In your OWN Batocera/SSH terminal, explicitly display the dedicated token:

   "$FW" alexa-info --show-token

   Its file is:

   /userdata/system/configs/flightwall/alexa-token.txt

   This is different from control-token.txt, Google OAuth files, and your
   release-signing key. Copy this Alexa token only into your own Lambda
   environment setting in Part 5. Do not send its value into the conversation.
   It authorizes only the limited voice household operations, not shell
   commands, app updates, calendar access, or general remote maintenance.


PART 4 — CREATE YOUR DEVELOPMENT SKILL

Do these browser steps on your normal computer. Use the Amazon account that
owns/registers the Echo device you want to use. Its Alexa app must use the
same account as your Alexa developer console account.

1. Open https://developer.amazon.com/alexa/console/ask and sign in. Complete
   the free developer-account profile if Amazon asks you to do so.

2. Choose Create Skill. Give it a display name such as Flight Wall Personal.
   Choose English (US) / en-US for this supplied interaction model. Your test
   device's Alexa language should match. Other locales need their own model
   and testing; merely changing the JSON filename does not add a locale.

3. Choose the Custom interaction model and Provision your own backend
   resources. If the console asks for a template, choose Start from Scratch.
   Do not choose a Smart Home model or Alexa-hosted backend for these steps.
   The exact screen order/wording can vary with console updates.

4. On the Build page, open Interaction Model > JSON Editor. Upload or paste
   the contents of Flight-Wall-Alexa-Interaction-Model-en-US.json. Use the
   supplied file itself, not this guide or an example request envelope.
   Save Model, then Build Model. Wait for a successful build.

5. Confirm the invocation name is:

   flight wall

   This is what you say after "Alexa, open" or "Alexa, tell". The skill's
   display name can differ. Keep the two-word invocation name for the supplied
   examples; if it conflicts with another skill in your account, choose a new
   valid invocation name and use that name when speaking.

6. Copy your Skill ID from the developer console. It has this shape:

   amzn1.ask.skill.YOUR_SKILL_ID

   You will use the exact ID in the Lambda trigger AND environment settings.
   Do not confuse it with an AWS account number, function ARN, or OAuth ID.

7. Leave account linking off for this private household skill. No Google
   sign-in belongs in Alexa's account-linking section for this integration.
   You do not need to enable contacts, location, calendar, or notification
   permissions for this feature.


PART 5 — CREATE THE SMALL AWS LAMBDA FUNCTION

1. Open https://console.aws.amazon.com/lambda/ in your own AWS account.
   Create an account if necessary and review its billing/free-tier terms.
   The Amazon developer console and AWS console are separate services even
   if you choose to use the same email address for both.

2. In the AWS region selector choose US East (N. Virginia), us-east-1,
   which is a supported Alexa Skills Kit Lambda region for North America.
   Then choose Create function > Author from scratch.

3. Use these values:

   Function name: FlightWallVoice
   Runtime:       Python 3.12, or a newer supported Python runtime
   Architecture:  x86_64
   Execution role: Create a new role with basic Lambda permissions

   This function makes outbound HTTPS requests. It does not need access to
   your Google account, AWS administrator permissions, API Gateway, a Lambda
   Function URL, DynamoDB, or an AWS VPC. Leave VPC attachment off for this
   simple setup so its normal outbound internet access remains available.

4. Create the function. On its Code tab, use Upload from > .zip file and
   select Flight-Wall-Alexa-Lambda-0.4.0.zip. Save/deploy the uploaded code.

   The supplied ZIP also includes timezone data so local dates work even if
   the cloud runtime lacks an IANA timezone database. There are no pip
   dependencies to install or Lambda layers to add. Use this complete ZIP
   for the novice setup. Pasting only the source file into the code editor
   requires a runtime with suitable IANA timezone data already available.

5. Under Runtime settings, choose Edit and set the handler to:

   lambda_function.lambda_handler

   If AWS says it cannot import lambda_function, the file was probably
   nested inside an extra ZIP folder. Use the supplied ready-to-upload ZIP.

6. Under Configuration > General configuration, use 128 MB memory and an
   8-second timeout. Alexa expects a quick response. Increasing Lambda's
   timeout does not make Alexa wait indefinitely for a slow/offline board.

7. Under Configuration > Environment variables > Edit, add these three
   entries. Enter raw values without surrounding quote characters:

   ALEXA_SKILL_ID
       Your exact amzn1.ask.skill... ID from Part 4.

   FLIGHTWALL_BASE_URL
       Your exact public HTTPS origin, for example
       https://flightwall.YOUR_TAILNET.ts.net
       No /mcp suffix, /api path, query string, or token in this value.

   FLIGHTWALL_ALEXA_TOKEN
       The dedicated token you viewed locally using alexa-info --show-token.
       This is the only secret you copy from the board into Lambda.

   Save. AWS stores environment settings separately from the uploaded code.
   Keep account access to these settings private. Do not paste the token
   into the code editor or any test event. The function sends it in an HTTPS
   Authorization header, not in a URL.

8. Add the invocation trigger from the Function overview panel:

   Add trigger > Alexa Skills Kit

   Enable Skill ID verification and enter the exact Skill ID from Part 4.
   Save the trigger. This adds the function permission for that specific
   skill. The supplied deployment-permission-example.json is a reference
   for administrators; using the console trigger is easier for this setup.
   Do not replace verification with an unrestricted function invocation URL.

9. Copy the function's ARN from the top of the Lambda page. Its shape is:

   arn:aws:lambda:us-east-1:YOUR_AWS_ACCOUNT:function:FlightWallVoice

   This ARN belongs in the Alexa developer console. It is not the board's
   HTTPS URL and does not contain the dedicated board token.


PART 6 — POINT THE SKILL AT LAMBDA AND TEST IT

1. Return to your Alexa skill in the developer console. On Build > Endpoint,
   choose AWS Lambda ARN. Put the exact function ARN from Part 5 into Default
   Region and Save Endpoints. This Alexa endpoint is Lambda, not the Tailscale
   address; Lambda is what talks to the board's HTTPS address.

2. Confirm the interaction model build succeeded. Open the Test tab and set
   Skill testing is enabled in to Development.

3. In the Alexa simulator, type:

   open flight wall skill

   Start with text testing so you can separate skill/backend setup from
   microphone recognition. Try these supported dinner interactions:

   tell flight wall we are having teriyaki chicken for dinner

   For a different day, say "set dinner for tomorrow," then answer the meal
   question with "dinner is teriyaki chicken." Free-form meal text and a date use separate conversation turns
   in this model. Not every arbitrary phrase containing both is supported.

4. Test an appointment with a common title:

   tell flight wall add a dentist appointment on November third at eleven twenty five a.m.

   Or open the skill, say "add an appointment called Emerson doctor
   appointment," then answer the date prompt with "on November third" and
   the time prompt with "at eleven twenty five a.m." When Alexa
   reads the proposed appointment back, say yes to save it or no to cancel.
   Use an explicit a.m./p.m. if the requested time could be ambiguous.

5. On Batocera, verify what was actually saved:

   "$FW" personal list
   "$FW" personal list --visible

   A successful save outside a card's display window will be in the first
   list but may not be in --visible yet. Dinner is displayed 14:30-19:00 by
   default; an appointment is displayed during its local date until its end.

6. Test your actual Echo using the same Amazon account and matching language:

   "Alexa, open Flight Wall skill."

   If the developer skill does not appear, check the Alexa app under
   More > Skills & Games > Your Skills > Dev Skills where available, and
   enable your development skill. Confirm the developer console is still
   set to Development testing. Menu wording can vary by app/device version.

7. Open Flight Wall from Ports on the TV and check the Personal view when
   the card is currently visible. If needed, use its settings/help controls
   to select or enable that view. The existing flight views remain available.

Do not publish this skill to the public Skill Store to make your own Echo
work. Your development skill is tied to your Amazon account; this source uses
one board credential and has no public multi-household account-linking design.


PART 7 — FIX THE MOST COMMON SETUP ERRORS

"I couldn't find that skill" or a general Alexa answer:
  Say the invocation name explicitly: "Alexa, open Flight Wall skill." Check the
  Amazon account on the Echo and developer console, en-US device/model locale,
  successful Build Model, and Development testing. Check for another skill
  with the same invocation name. Ordinary "Alexa, add an appointment" can
  invoke Amazon's calendar feature instead of your custom skill.

"The requested skill's response is invalid" / endpoint error:
  Confirm the Lambda ARN, us-east-1 region, Alexa Skills Kit trigger, exact
  Skill ID verification, and handler lambda_function.lambda_handler. Verify
  you uploaded the right ZIP and clicked Deploy after code-editor changes.

The skill opens but cannot save to the board:
  Check the board's power/internet, flightwall service, flightwall_tailscale
  service, and public /health page. Confirm Funnel is public, not private
  Serve. Compare FLIGHTWALL_BASE_URL with the real HTTPS address, and confirm
  alexa_enabled=true. A wrong/rotated token will be rejected. Do not weaken
  TLS verification or put the token in the URL to work around an error.

The simulator reaches the wrong skill or uses old words:
  Rebuild the model after changing its JSON. Disable conflicting development
  skills with the same invocation name. Confirm you edited the en-US model
  and Lambda function currently referenced by this skill's endpoint ARN.

An appointment title/date/time was misunderstood:
  Use the guided conversation and listen to the confirmation before saying
  yes. Free-form appointment titles are captured in a separate turn from
  date/time because of Alexa slot-model constraints. Use a specific date
  and a.m./p.m. instead of a broad range such as "sometime next week."

The item saved but the TV shows no card:
  Check personal_enabled, board timezone, chosen date, and --visible output.
  An appointment is no longer shown after its end; dinner is shown only in
  the dinner window. Restart/open the display after initial setup. A future
  dinner card remains saved until its display date/window arrives.

The time is off by several hours or dinner lands on the wrong date:
  Correct the board's IANA timezone, system date/time, and current defaults.
  The function reads those from the board rather than using its own UTC day.

Duplicate delivery/retry:
  The function supplies Alexa's request ID and the board deduplicates retries.
  Do not treat a timeout as proof the write failed; check personal list before
  speaking a different request. A new dinner for the same date replaces the
  prior dinner, and an old retry must not bring a replaced meal back.

AWS diagnosis:
  In your Lambda function, use Monitor > View CloudWatch logs for its own
  invocation errors. Review logs privately and redact personal information
  before sharing an error. Do not log complete voice requests, tokens, or
  environment variables while troubleshooting. Live Amazon speech/NLU and
  your deployed AWS resources were not tested by the local application tests.


PART 8 — TURN ALEXA OFF OR REPLACE ITS TOKEN

To disable this voice integration while leaving the app and ChatGPT/Codex
connection available, close the Ports display and run on Batocera:

   batocera-services stop flightwall
   "$FW" config --set alexa_enabled false
   batocera-services start flightwall

To replace the dedicated token, stop the service/close the display first:

   batocera-services stop flightwall
   "$FW" config --set alexa_enabled false
   rm -f /userdata/system/configs/flightwall/alexa-token.txt
   "$FW" alexa-info --show-token

Copy the newly generated token into your OWN Lambda environment setting
FLIGHTWALL_ALEXA_TOKEN and save it. Then re-enable the integration:

   "$FW" config --set alexa_enabled true
   batocera-services start flightwall

Do not delete control-token.txt, google-token.json, your whole data directory,
or any release-signing key for this operation. They are separate credentials.

If you no longer want the cloud skill, turn its Development testing off and
remove it from your Alexa account. Delete the specific FlightWallVoice Lambda
function if you no longer use it, and review any retained CloudWatch logs or
other AWS resources in that account. If Funnel existed only for this project,
you may reset it using the commands in INSTALL.txt. Resetting a shared Funnel
also disconnects other remote app clients using that same address.


TECHNICAL REFERENCE AND LIMITS

Lambda only uses the dedicated voice endpoints:

  GET  /api/v1/voice-context
       Board timezone and dinner/appointment defaults; no flight roster.

  POST /api/v1/personal-items
       Validated dinner/appointment creation with source=alexa and a request ID.

  GET  /api/v1/timers
  POST /api/v1/timers
  POST /api/v1/timers/cancel
       Only household timer state, bounded timer starts and ID-scoped dismissal.

  GET  /api/v1/shopping-list
  POST /api/v1/shopping-list/add
  POST /api/v1/shopping-list/remove
  POST /api/v1/shopping-list/clear
  POST /api/v1/shopping-list/show
       Only Flight Wall list state and bounded changes; clearing requires an
       explicit confirmation. Show controls the list's TV ticker visibility.

  POST /api/v1/flight-watches
       Airline/number/date request and safe public watch acknowledgement only.
       No operating-calendar, roster, mail, settings or administrator access.

The dedicated token is not a Google credential and does not authorize the MCP
tools. Alexa/Amazon processes what you speak, and your AWS function receives
the resulting structured request. The board stores the resulting local card.
These local cards do not synchronize to Google Calendar, create Alexa's own
calendar events, or send proactive spoken reminders. This integration adds
cards to your Flight Wall display.

Verification results and remaining limitations are recorded in VALIDATION.txt.
Source tests are not a new native Linux build or proof that the new model
builds in Amazon, new commands recognize speech, or 0.4.0 runs on the TV.
The installed 0.3.0 onboarding checks do not validate these new capabilities.
